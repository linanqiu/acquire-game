# LO-012: No suicide mergers; bot generations compared in self-play

## Metadata
- **Epic**: [LLM Opponents](../../epics/09-llm-bot.md)
- **Status**: `completed`
- **Priority**: `high`
- **Effort**: `L`
- **Dependencies**: ES-004 (calculator pack), LO-009 (strategy playbook), LO-010 (trade valuation)

## Context

Owner (2026-11-17), with a phone screenshot from a live game: "Bots keep doing suicide merge moves
like this." A rules-based bot placed a tile that merged a 3-tile chain into a 4-tile one while
holding none of the smaller chain: the merger paid one rival $4,000, the human $2,000 and the bot
nothing. Then: "Try a few loops of this. Propose new bot rules, keep old versions and Monte Carlo
who wins. And then add new rules."

Root cause, tile value, pick: [docs/ai/bot-tiles.md](../../../ai/bot-tiles.md).
Generations, tournament and results: [docs/ai/bot-versions.md](../../../ai/bot-versions.md).

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] A position-aware tile value (`game/tile_value.py`), deterministic, public information plus
      the bot's own tile, reusing the engine's placement, survivor and bonus rules and the trade
      valuation's bonus weights: every player's gain (merger bonuses in full, trade surplus in the
      engine's disposition order, survivor/chain growth in price and weighted bonus, a lock bonus
      when a chain becomes safe, founding) and score = my gain - rival-weighted rival gains.
- [x] A merger that pays only rivals scores clearly negative; one that pays the bot the majority
      while rivals get less stays positive; founding and growing on the same money scale.
- [x] Pick: hard = best; medium = random among tiles within $100 of the best, never a negative
      tile while a non-negative one exists; all tiles harmful = least harmful; easy unchanged.
- [x] Tier 1 (`tests/test_tile_value.py`, real-play positions): the screenshot situation across
      40 RNG seeds on medium and hard; bot holds the defunct majority; bot holds the survivor
      only; all-harmful hand; easy unchanged; medium still varies; the valuation's bonuses equal
      the engine's.
- [x] LLM seats: playbook sentence and a "pays only rivals" mark on merging tiles;
      `PROMPT_BUILDER_VERSION` 9 -> 10 (merged after the merger-order prompt, which took 9); `docs/ai/strategy-playbook.md`.
- [x] Versioned bots (`agents/bot_versions.py`, `BotPolicy`): v1 (exactly the old bot: same
      action sequences as `main` on 16 fixed-seed games) and every later version stay playable
      (`bot:vN`); default = strongest accepted (v8); smoke test per version and difficulty
      (`tests/test_bot_versions.py`); rule tests (`tests/test_bot_rules.py`).
- [x] Tournament harness (`bench/bot_tournament.py`): parallel, fixed seeds, 2v2 and 1v3 seatings,
      mixed 3-6 seat tables, win/rank/margin with 95 % intervals, paired-by-seed test, behaviour
      profile; tiny-run test.
- [x] Generation loops: v2, v3, v8 accepted; v4-v7 rejected with numbers; one more loop of
      screens over v8 found nothing clear. Final numbers on seeds never used in development.
- [x] Fixtures unchanged (`python -m fixtures_gen`: the generator does not use bots); full
      Playwright suite green.

## Notes

- **Where the tile value lives.** Unlike the trade valuation, it is computed by the bot itself
  from the public state it already rebuilds (all players' cash and shares are public), so the
  calculator pack is unchanged.
- **Board copy.** `Board.__deepcopy__` now copies only the cells and shares the (frozen) ruleset:
  the calculator simulates every tile in hand with a board copy, and the generic deep copy was
  about 90 % of a bot game's time (bot games about 10x faster; the server's calculator pack gets
  the same speed-up).
- **Deviation:** suicide merges are not zero: the rest are forced (every tile merges or the
  alternatives hurt more) or pay the bot through the survivor. Judged on the numbers, as asked.
- **Tests adjusted to new bot behaviour, none weakened:** the LLM prompt tests' fixture position is
  played by `bot:v1` (its numbers were checked by hand for that position); the mini-server test's
  human founds when it can (the stronger bots found first); the trade protocol test uses deal 2
  (a deal where a bot leads a chain the human can afford); the calculator disposition test now
  separates the viewer's own sale proceeds from bonuses a later defunct chain of a 3-chain merger
  pays in the same call (a position the new bots reach on `mini`; the old check summed both).
  Journeys: the mobile journey plays its 8 turns and then on (hard cap 30) until the disposition
  screen and the trade have come up, instead of assuming they happen within 8 turns; the
  two-humans journey seats `bot:v1` from the AI menu (on the fixed deal, v1's play leads to the
  tied merger that exercises Bob's survivor choice; with v8 the bot declares the end after 19
  turns each without one) and loops with a hard cap until a survivor choice and a disposition
  have happened.
- **Test-mode AI menu:** every non-default generation is offered as `bot:vN` when
  `GAME_TEST_MODE=1` (like "Scripted"); production menus are unchanged. Surface map PL-02 updated.
- Bench: bots are the bench baseline (`bot` = default version), so bench results from before
  this story are not comparable with results after it. `bot:v1` reproduces the old baseline.
- Story id: LO-012 was the next free id in this epic on 2026-11-17; a parallel branch adding a
  "choose the order of tied defunct chains" decision may also touch `bot.py` / `heuristic.py` and
  the prompt version.
