# LO-009: Strategy playbook for LLM seats

## Metadata
- **Epic**: [LLM Opponents](../../epics/09-llm-bot.md)
- **Status**: `completed`
- **Priority**: `high`
- **Effort**: `L` (2-4h)
- **Dependencies**: LO-002 (prompt builder), ES-004 (calculator pack), AB-003 (bench protocol)

## Context

Owner (2026-11-15): "The LLMs have quite a bit of trouble strategizing, so you may need to make a
strategy rubric or something like a crash course for it. Otherwise it keeps making stupid
mistakes."

Evidence first: find the mistakes LLM seats actually make in benchmark transcripts, fix them with
guidance and better numbers (no extra model calls, no higher effort or token limits), and measure
the change on the same games. Analysis, before/after table and how to re-run:
[docs/ai/strategy-playbook.md](../../../ai/strategy-playbook.md).

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] Baseline: Claude Haiku 5.5, `bench-v1` vs-baseline, 24 games, transcripts kept; win rate,
      mean rank, score margin, retries, fallbacks, tokens and cost recorded.
- [x] Blunders measured from the transcripts (replayed through the engine with the calculator
      pack at each decision) and ranked by frequency and cost, with the model's own `why` lines.
- [x] The system prompt's "Strategy guidance" is replaced by a compact playbook: core principles,
      a checklist per decision type, named blunders, guidance for the private note. Theme-neutral:
      tested under `valley` and `mini` (no default chain names, theme words or numbers; only the
      ruleset's own multipliers and limits appear).
- [x] New calculator numbers, each derived by the engine and tier-1 tested against what the engine
      then does in played positions: the share race per chain (lead, shares a rival needs to pass,
      locked majorities), every player's merger bonuses per merge tile, and the merger disposition
      (sell vs trade at the survivor's post-merger price, survivor standing after trading).
- [x] The per-decision prompt renders those numbers; prompt tests check them against the pack.
- [x] Same 24 games re-run with the playbook; before/after table and blunder counts documented;
      one iteration on the weakest area. `PROMPT_BUILDER_VERSION` bumped once (4 -> 5).
- [x] Kimi K3 (low effort) checked before and after on the same games (small sample).
- [x] No change to the rules-based bot, engine rules, wire formats, table talk or `bench-v1`.

## Notes

- Deviation: the story asked for "a one-line 'what this buys you' per purchase option". The
  purchase lines already carried standing after and payout change; the missing piece in the
  transcripts was how safe a lead is, so the share race went on the chain lines instead (shown in
  every decision kind, not only buys).
- The baseline model already beat the rules-based bots (50% wins over 24 games), so the mistakes
  are the gap between "beats a weak bot" and good play, not illegal or random moves.
- Bench output and keys are not committed; the analysis script is throwaway (its definitions are
  in the playbook doc).
