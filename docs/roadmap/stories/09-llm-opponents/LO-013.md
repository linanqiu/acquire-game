# LO-013: Trade talk that makes sense

## Metadata
- **Epic**: [LLM Opponents](../../epics/09-llm-bot.md)
- **Status**: `completed`
- **Priority**: `high`
- **Effort**: `M`
- **Dependencies**: LO-008 (table chat), LO-010 (trade valuation), LO-011 (talk calls gated by rules)

## Context

Owner (2026-11-18): "See the UMWT game. The AI Haiku seems to be saying nonsensical things about
trade." In that live game the Claude Haiku 5.5 seat read "If I give you SP you can get ahead of
lil John" backwards, put every deal off to "my turn" and never sent an offer, claimed to have
founded a chain it had not, talked about itself in the third person, and answered an offer it had
just rejected about shares the offer never asked for.

Root causes, the new rules and facts, and the before/after replies:
[docs/ai/table-talk.md](../../../ai/table-talk.md).

## Root causes

1. Talk rules said "say you will make or accept the trade offer on your turn, and then do it":
   false (offers are answered when they arrive; the seat proposes only at its buy step) and nothing
   followed up at the buy step, whose prompt did not even name `propose_trade`.
2. No computed facts about a deal under discussion: direction and value worked out from prose.
3. The talk prompt's event list is truncated; questions about the game's history were answered by
   guessing.
4. "Players may call you 'Hedge'" with no first-person rule.
5. Trade events rendered as a raw dict cut off before the money; the offer line ("offers you: A
   in exchange for: B") was easy to garble.
6. (found here) An unnamed follow-up in a one-to-one exchange was labelled "to the table" and
   routed like a table message.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] Talk rules match the mechanics: offers any time, answered on arrival; the seat proposes only
      at its own buy step; no "on your turn" for answering. Rule: "I give you X" means you receive
      X. First person, never its own name. Only facts shown; never guess about earlier play. A
      person talking to the seat gets an answer.
- [x] "Deal facts" in the talk prompt only when the trigger needs them (a chain named, a deal
      word, an open or just-answered offer between the two): for each chain, 1-3 shares each way
      with the seat's count, top rival, standing change, bonus change and value, from
      `game.trade_value.trade_valuation`; offers as "you GIVE / you GET".
- [x] "Who did what this game" in every in-game talk prompt: chains founded, mergers set off,
      trades, current majorities and minorities per player, from the full event log.
- [x] Decision prompt: trade events as sentences; the offer as "If you accept: you GIVE ... and
      you GET ...; you have $N cash" with a rule for `say`; the buy step names `propose_trade`;
      a "Deal talk since your last turn" block (only when there was deal talk) tells the seat to
      send what it promised or say it will not, with the direction mapping to the action fields.
      `PROMPT_BUILDER_VERSION` 10 -> 11 (talk has no separate version; the constant covers it).
- [x] An unnamed message that continues a one-to-one exchange goes to that seat and is shown as
      "(to you, after your last message)".
- [x] Tier 1 (`tests/test_llm_trade_talk.py`, positions reached by bot play): rules text, deal
      facts direction and rank effects checked against the engine's standings, no facts for small
      talk, open and rejected offers from the seat's side, history counts, trade event lines,
      offer line, buy-step block and when it is absent. Tier 2 (`tests/protocol/test_chat.py`):
      follow-up routing and the prompt label; `talk_view` deal facts only for deal talk.
- [x] Real-model check, Claude Haiku 5.5 only, 3 samples per point, old (saved) vs new (rebuilt
      from the replayed UMWT state) prompts for the talk replies at entries 459, 462, 472, 475,
      511, 740, the trade answers at 733 and 743 and the buy steps at 491 and 532: table in
      docs/ai/table-talk.md. Spent $0.147 in all.
- [x] Fixtures unchanged (`python -m fixtures_gen`); frontend untouched.

## Known limits

- The decision prompt has no "Deal facts" block (the decision context carries no game; adding
  it would touch the calculator pack's per-broadcast path). Follow-up if buy-step proposals stay
  confused.
- Haiku still garbles some replies; the facts make the right answer available, not certain.
