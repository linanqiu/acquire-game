# BL-012: Public trade offers: everyone sees them and can make a competing offer

## Metadata
- **Epic**: Backlog
- **Status**: `completed`
- **Priority**: `high`
- **Effort**: `M` (1-2 hours)
- **Dependencies**: BL-008 (game-page tabs), BL-011 (decision tab)

## Context

Owner request (2026-11-16): "Trade proposals should be public so that everyone can see them and
possibly propose other proposals."

Before: `game_state` already listed every open offer (`pending_trades`), but the player page
showed only the offers a player was part of, and the notifications (`trade_proposed`,
`trade_accepted`, `trade_rejected`, `trade_canceled`) went to the two parties and the host only.
A third player could not see that a deal was on the table, and so could not react to it.

## Acceptance Criteria
- [x] **Backend:** the four trade notifications go to the whole room (every player and the host),
      one code path for every seat kind (`broadcast.trade_message`, derived from the engine
      event). Each carries the full `trade`, `trade_id`, `from_player`, `to_player` and `seq`;
      answers also `rejected_by` / `canceled_by` (and `reason` on a cancel). The engine's
      `trade_rejected` / `trade_cancelled` events now carry the offer too. `game_state` repeats the
      last 12 messages in `trade_log`.
- [x] **Still only the parties act:** only the recipient accepts or rejects, only the proposer
      cancels (engine, unchanged); tested in tier 1 and through the WebSocket.
- [x] **Players see every open offer** in the Trades tab: my own (cancel), then "Open offers at
      the table": offers for me (with ANSWER, which opens the decision tab) and offers between
      other players, read-only, with who offers what to whom.
- [x] **Competing offer:** from an offer between two other players, "Make a competing offer"
      TO <recipient> / TO <proposer> opens the trade builder on that player with the terms filled
      in as a starting point (to the recipient: what the proposer offered for what they asked; to
      the proposer: what they asked for what they offered), cut down to what each side holds. It
      is an ordinary proposal; the first offer stays open.
- [x] **Signal, not noise:** a new offer between two other players is one info toast ("Cara
      offers Bob a trade"; several at once: one toast with the count) and a dot on the Trades tab
      until the tab has been opened. Their answers are not toasted. An offer for me still counts
      on the tab and opens the decision tab as before.
- [x] **Activity in words, with terms**, for everyone: `lib/eventText.ts: tradeEventText` ("Cara
      offers Bob 2 Aurelia for $800.", "Bob accepted Cara's offer: ...", "Bob rejected ...",
      "Cara withdrew the offer to Bob: ..."). Used by the transcript timeline, the player's
      "Recent trades" list and the host's Log.
- [x] **AI seats:** the rules text says offers are public and anyone may make a competing offer;
      the `buy_stocks` prompt lists open offers between other players with both sides valued at
      current prices. `PROMPT_BUILDER_VERSION` 5 -> 6. No new model calls: an AI seat competes
      only through `propose_trade` on its own buy decision. The table-talk prompt is unchanged.
- [x] **Docs:** `docs/rules/gameplay.md` (public offers, competing offers), the seat contract,
      the surface coverage map (TR-08..TR-11, HO-12, fixture `trade-between-others`).
- [x] **Phones:** 360x640 and 390x844 (and 1366x768) show the offer, the buttons and the builder
      without horizontal scrolling.
- [x] **Tests:** tier 1 `test_events.py` (events carry the offer; outsiders cannot answer),
      `test_llm_prompt.py` (third-party offer in the prompt, rules text, version); tier 2

## Implementation Notes

- **One message builder.** `broadcast.trade_message(game, event)` turns a trade event into the
  public message; `publish_events` sends it to the room and the host, and `trade_log(game)` puts
  the last 12 into `game_state`. An answer recorded before events carried the offer (an old
  snapshot) falls back to the `trade_proposed` event.
- **Events are keyed by the engine's `seq`** in the store (`tradeLog`), so a message and the same
  entry in a later state's `trade_log` are one event. Entries first learned from the first state
  are history (`live: false`): listed, never toasted. Entries learned from a later state were
  missed while the socket was down and are announced then.
- **Several events in one render.** The page used to look at the newest event only; with an AI
  seat answering at once, `trade_proposed` and its answer can arrive together, and the first was
  then skipped (no "Offer sent" toast, the builder stayed open). `usePlayerTrades` now handles
  every event after the last announced one, in order.
- **Why the two "scripted opponent accepts / rejects" screen tests failed now and then:** the
  test loaded the snapshot through the API as soon as the page URL changed. Under load that
  could be before the page's WebSocket was open, so the scripted seat's answer was sent to no
  socket of this page and no toast could follow. `startFromSnapshot` now waits until the lobby
  lists every seat (that list arrives over the socket) before loading the snapshot.
- `lib/trades.ts: competingOffer` is a pure function; the builder takes `initial` and is
  remounted by key for each opening.

### Deviations from the brief
- **Offers for me stay in the decision tab** (BL-011). The Trades tab lists them too, with an
  ANSWER button that opens the decision tab, instead of a second set of ACCEPT / REJECT buttons.
- **Buttons are "TO <name>"** under the label "Make a competing offer" (two names fit a 360px
  phone; "Offer something else" as a second label did not).
- **The open-offers block is in the `buy_stocks` prompt only**, the one decision where an AI seat
  can propose a trade. Trade events were already in every prompt's recent events.
- **Table talk unchanged.** Its prompt already shows recent public events; adding offers there
  risked more chatter, which the owner asked to keep down.
- **`trade_log` in `game_state`** was not in the brief; it makes the recent-trades list survive a
  reload and lets a page catch up after a dropped connection.

## Follow-ups (not done)
